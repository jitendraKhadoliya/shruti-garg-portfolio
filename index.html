01 Lead generation · Meta Ads

The architecture & interior lead engine

High-ticket architecture and interior firms needed a dependable flow of qualified enquiries — not cheap clicks that never turn into projects. I ran the paid media end-to-end: structure, audiences, creative direction, optimization and reporting.

Role
Sole media buyer
Challenge
Hold cost-per-lead down while keeping lead quality high enough to convert into high-value projects.
VERIFIED OUTCOME
1,500+ qualified leads
Cost-per-lead held between ₹73 and ₹166 across campaigns through continuous A/B testing and audience refinement.

Context

Architecture, interior-design, home-renovation and modular-kitchen businesses selling projects worth lakhs. A single client is worth far more than a single lead, so the whole account is judged on lead quality, not lead volume.

Key decisions

  • —Split prospecting, remarketing and lookalike audiences into separate ad sets so every budget line had one job and one number to answer for.
  • —Ran a low-cost awareness layer (3.5M+ reach at ₹2.31 CPM) to feed the lead campaigns warmer audiences.
  • —Added qualifying questions to the lead form to filter tyre-kickers before they reached the client's sales team.

Difficult edge cases

Two ad sets in one account diverged hard — one at ₹73.94 per lead, another at ₹136.50. Rather than average them, I rebalanced budget toward the efficient set and rebuilt the expensive one's targeting, keeping the blended cost-per-lead in control.

Result

Consistent, qualified enquiries fed a real high-value project pipeline — including a multi-lakh retainer engagement, with the first client already converted and others in progress.

What I'd improve next

Closer feedback from the client's sales team on which leads actually booked consultations — so optimization targets closed projects, not just form fills.

02 E-commerce sales · Meta Ads

Profitable purchases for a US luxury footwear brand

A US brand selling handcrafted shoes at a ~$250 average order value needed website purchases at a cost that stayed profitable — not just cheap traffic. I built the sales campaigns, prospecting audiences and creative tests, and managed budget toward purchase volume.

Role
Media buyer
Challenge
Drive purchases below Meta's category benchmark while protecting margin on a high-AOV product.
VERIFIED OUTCOME
53% below benchmark
159 website purchases across sales campaigns at $10.35–$15.64 each — versus a peer median of $31.46 per purchase.

Context

Luxury handcrafted footwear sold direct-to-consumer in the US. With a ~$250 order value, a single sale can absorb real ad cost — but only if the account is optimized toward purchases, not clicks or add-to-carts.

Key decisions

  • —Optimized directly for the purchase event via the pixel, not upper-funnel proxies, so the algorithm chased revenue.
  • —Ran prospecting and interest-based ad sets in parallel and shifted budget toward whichever held cost-per-purchase lowest.
  • —Rotated creative continuously to fight fatigue on a small, high-intent US audience.

03 Brand launch · Full-funnel

Lakwin Jewels — a brand launched from zero

A new jewelry brand with no website, no ad infrastructure and no presence. As freelance marketer and WordPress developer, I stood up the entire commercial funnel — and made the first spend count.

Role
Freelance — marketer + web
Challenge
Build a full funnel from nothing — site, tracking, ads and organic — as one person.

Context

Most media buyers inherit an account that already works. Here there was nothing — no site, no pixel, no audiences, no content. Every layer of the funnel had to be created before a single ad could run.

What I owned

  • —Designed and built the WordPress site with conversion and UX in mind.
  • —Set up Meta Business Manager, Pixel and event tracking from scratch.
  • —Planned the Meta strategy, wrote the ad copy and ran campaigns end-to-end.
  • —Built the organic social foundation to support paid reach.

Why it matters

This is the project that proves range: paid media doesn't live in isolation. Knowing how the site, tracking and creative fit together makes every campaign I run afterwards more reliable — because I understand what happens after the click.

What I'd improve next

Formal conversion tracking and a reporting cadence from day one, so early spend is measured as tightly as a mature account.

About

I treat an ad budget the way an accountant treats a ledger: every rupee and dollar has a job, and it should be able to show what it did.

I'm a performance marketer based in Mohali, India, working across Meta and Google Ads for lead-gen and e-commerce brands in India, Australia and the US. I like the problems where the numbers are messy — a cost-per-lead that won't come down, an account that scales and then stalls — because that's where structure and testing actually earn their keep.

A finance-and-marketing background means I'm comfortable with the commercial side too: what a lead is worth, when to spend and when to hold. Having also built sites, pixels and tracking from scratch, I understand what happens after the click — which makes the campaigns I run more honest and more reliable.

Next, I want bigger budgets and sharper problems — high-ticket lead-gen and e-commerce accounts, in a team that reports clearly and measures what matters.
